# Go-to-market plan: the first 90 days

## The goal

**Ten trainers with players training every week, by day 90.** Not sign-ups, not page
views. A trainer counts when at least five of their players have joined and at least
half of them finish a session in a given week.

Why ten: it is enough to know what trainers actually use, to have three or four
quotes and one video, and to see whether trainers bring other trainers. It is also
about what one person can set up by hand.

## Who it's for

**The trainer you want:**

- Works for themselves, with players from different clubs
- Has 8 to 40 regular players, in private or small-group sessions
- Already gives homework somehow: texts, YouTube links, a word to the parent at pickup
- Posts drills on Instagram, so they have videos and care how they look to parents

**Not yet:** clubs, team coaches, directors of coaching. They buy slowly, want team
features you haven't built, and the whole site is written for the independent
trainer. Say no politely and keep their name.

## What you say

> You see each player about once a week. Train.futbol is for the other six days. You
> pick drills for your players to do at home, and you see who did them.

Three reasons a trainer says yes, in the order they care:

1. **Their players improve faster**, which is what parents pay them for.
2. **They look more professional**: their own page, their name and colors, a plan
   parents can see.
3. **It pays for itself.** A session costs $80 to $125, so one or two extra sessions
   a year from a player cover what this costs for them.

## The offer

**What you say:** $10 a player a month, or $5 a player if the trainer would rather
charge their players their own way. Every player's first 7 days are free. No setup
fee, no contract. This is the wording on the site, the deck and the emails.

**How each is paid** (for the conversation, not the pitch):

- **At $10,** the player's family pays Train.futbol by card after the free days, and
  the trainer pays nothing. Families can cancel any time.
- **At $5,** the trainer pays for the players on their page and charges them however
  they like, or includes it in their fees. This suits clubs and teams.

**Founding trainers:** the first 3 are set up by you personally and get their first 3
months free. Where families pay, that means the families' first 3 months. In return the trainer gives
honest feedback and, if they're happy, a quote.

Once the three places are taken, take the 3-months line out of the emails, the deck
and the ads.

**Not offered yet:** trainers setting their own price for families, or earning a
share. Don't promise either.

## The plan

### Days 1 to 7: get ready

Work through the checklist in `launch-kit.md`. The ones that matter most:

- Check the site's pricing says the same as "The offer" above (see "Before you
  launch" below).
- Record the 90-second screen video of a trainer's week.
- Pick one inbox for trainers to reply to, and set it on the site.
- Start the tracking sheet.

### Days 8 to 21: trainer zero

One trainer, fully live. Start with the trainer you already work with.

- Set up their page with them: three or four of their real sessions, their videos.
- Sit with them while they add their first players and send the link.
- Give them the message to send parents (it's in `launch-kit.md`).
- Watch for two weeks. How many players join? How many finish a session? Where do
  they get stuck? Fix what you find before anyone else sees it.
- At the end, ask for a quote, a 20-second video, and two introductions.

Do not skip ahead. Everything after this is easier with one trainer who can say
"I use it and my players do the work".

### Days 22 to 60: the first ten

Direct outreach, ten new trainers contacted every weekday.

**Build a list of 100.** Sources, best first:

1. Introductions from trainer zero and from soccer parents you know
2. Instagram: search "[your city] soccer training", "private soccer trainer", and the
   accounts those trainers follow
3. Google Maps: "private soccer training near [city]"
4. Trainer listings such as CoachUp, and the "private training" pages of local clubs
5. Trainers you see working at local fields and indoor facilities. Walk up and ask.

**Reach out** with the messages in `trainer-emails.md`. Instagram messages get more
replies from trainers than email does; use email where you have an address, and follow
up twice.

**What to expect.** Rough numbers to plan around, not promises: of 100 trainers
contacted, 20 to 30 reply, 10 to 15 take a demo, 6 to 10 ask for a page, 4 to 6 end up
active. If your reply rate is under 10% after the first 30, change the first line of
the message before sending more.

**Demo, then set up within 48 hours.** Interest fades fast. Use the demo script in
`launch-kit.md` and book the setup call before the demo ends.

### Days 61 to 90: make it repeat

- **Referrals.** Ask every active trainer for two introductions. Trainers pay
  nothing now, so a free month means nothing to them; offer to feature them on the
  Train.futbol Instagram, and decide later whether a paid reward is worth it.
  Trainers know other trainers.
- **Proof on the site.** Put the first quotes and the trainer video on /trainers, and
  replace the "What it does for you" slide in the deck with real results.
- **Content.** Three Instagram posts a week from a Train.futbol account (plan in
  `launch-kit.md`). Tag the trainers; they repost, and other trainers see it.
- **The players' side.** Each trainer page is seen by 10 to 40 families. A small
  "Train.futbol" line at the bottom of a trainer's page, and the Find your trainer
  page, let other trainers' players ask their trainer for it.
- **Ads.** Start the small test in `ads.md` once trainer zero is on camera.
- **Watch the first payments.** For trainers four to ten, families reach the end of
  their 7 free days during this stretch. The share who subscribe is the truest number
  you will get. The founding trainers' families are free for 3 months, so their first
  payments fall after day 90.

## Channels, ranked

| # | Channel | Cost | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introductions and people you know | Time | Highest yes rate by far |
| 2 | Instagram messages to trainers | Time | Where trainers already show their work |
| 3 | In person at fields and facilities | Time | A phone demo in two minutes |
| 4 | Email to trainers | Time | Slower, but it scales to a list |
| 5 | Trainer referrals | A feature on your Instagram | Starts once trainers are happy |
| 6 | Instagram content | Time | Proof for everyone you contact |
| 7 | Paid ads | $200 to start | Only after there's proof to show |
| 8 | Facebook groups, r/bootroom, coaching forums | Time | Share what you learned, don't pitch |

## What to measure

Keep one sheet, updated every Friday.

| Number | What it tells you | Aim by day 90 |
|---|---|---|
| Trainers contacted | Whether you're doing the work | 150 |
| Demos given | Whether the message works | 25 |
| Pages set up | Whether the demo works | 15 |
| **Active trainers** | Whether the product works | **10** |
| Players joined per trainer | Whether trainers can get players on | 8 or more |
| Players finishing a session each week | Whether players come back | Half of those joined |
| Share of a trainer's players whose families subscribe after the free days | Whether it's a business | 45% |
| Families who cancel each month | Whether the income lasts | 5% or fewer |

The one to watch most closely is **sessions finished per week**. If trainers sign up
but players don't train, more marketing won't help. Fix that first.

## A weekly rhythm

- **Monday:** look at last week's numbers. Message any trainer whose players went quiet.
- **Tuesday to Thursday:** ten new trainers contacted each day. Demos and setups.
- **Friday:** one improvement to the product from what trainers said. Update the sheet.
- **Any day:** one Instagram post.

About six to eight hours a week, outside of product work.

## Before you launch

Things I found in the project that affect what you can promise:

1. **Family billing is being built now.** Families paying $10 a month by card is the
   main way you earn, and it isn't live yet. Don't send a trainer's families a join
   link that promises it until it has been tested end to end. Check that /trainers
   and /players state the new price; the old line about players paying trainers
   through Train.futbol is dropped.
2. **Trainer-pays billing is built and tested, in Stripe's test mode.** This is the
   $5 a player option for a trainer who'd rather cover it. Before any real payment,
   clear the test records and switch to live keys.
3. **The privacy and terms pages haven't been reviewed by a lawyer**, and under-13
   sign-up by a parent isn't enforced (both are on your to-do list). With families
   paying by card, a parent has to be the one who signs up and pays, and the terms
   need to cover payments, cancelling and refunds. Do the review
   before families you don't know start joining, which is exactly what this plan
   brings.
4. **Hosting plans.** Your launch notes say the free hosting tier is for
   non-commercial use only and that the free database tier pauses when idle. Check
   which plans you're on before a trainer you don't know depends on it.
5. **The demo page uses the licensed drill library.** Showing it in a demo is what
   it's for. Keep it out of ads and out of new trainers' pages.
6. **Setting up each trainer by hand is the plan, not a problem,** until about ten.
   Past that, the setup steps need to work without you.
