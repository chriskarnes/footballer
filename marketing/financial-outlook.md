# Financial outlook: the first 12 months

This follows `go-to-market.md`: ten active trainers by day 90, three of them founding
trainers, and steady growth after that. Month 1 is December 2026.

Every figure here is built from assumptions, listed below. They are planning numbers,
not predictions. By day 90 you will have your own numbers for the ones that matter
most, and you should replace mine with them.

**Revised 15 November 2026 for the new price.** Trainers pay nothing. Families pay
Train.futbol directly: $10 a month per player, by card, after 7 days free. A trainer
who would rather include it can cover it at $5 a player. The earlier versions of this
document, built on trainers paying $5 a player, no longer apply.

## The short version

- **You earn from families, not trainers.** About $9.31 of each $10 is kept after the
  card fee and running costs.
- **It is cheap to run.** About $50 a month, covered by six paying families.
- **You risk about $1,100 to find out.** That is the deepest point in the expected
  case, mostly a legal review and the ad test.
- **In the expected case it pays that back in month 4** and ends the year about
  $30,900 ahead, taking in about $5,500 a month by month 12.
- **In the conservative case it ends the year about $2,400 ahead.**
- **$20,000 a month takes 2,000 paying players**, which is about 99 trainers at 20
  paying families each.
- **The number everything rests on is new:** the share of a trainer's players whose
  families subscribe once the free days end. Nobody knows it yet.

## How many families pay, per trainer

A trainer no longer decides how many players you are paid for. Each family does.

| | Conservative | Expected | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Players a trainer works with | 30 | 45 | 45 |
| Share whose families subscribe after the free days | 25% | 45% | 60% |
| Paying players per trainer | 7.5 | about 20 | 27 |

Three cautions:

- **The share is a guess.** Free trials that end in a card payment can convert well
  below half. A trainer telling families "this is part of training with me" should do
  better than a cold sign-up, but 45% is still an assumption.
- **Rosters of 30 to 60 come from two groups you know.** Solo trainers may have 10
  to 25, which is why the conservative case uses 30 and a low share.
- **Transient players are your cancellations now.** The model treats paying players
  per trainer as steady: families who cancel are replaced by new players joining the
  same trainer. If cancellations run ahead of new players, that number shrinks.

## One trainer

Trainers pay nothing, so what a trainer is worth to you is their paying families.

| A trainer with | Families pay you | Card fees | Other cost | You keep |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 paying families | $80 | $4.72 | $0.80 | $74.48 |
| 14 paying families | $140 | $8.26 | $1.40 | $130.34 |
| 20 paying families | $200 | $11.80 | $2.00 | $186.20 |
| 27 paying families | $270 | $15.93 | $2.70 | $251.37 |
| 36 paying families | $360 | $21.24 | $3.60 | $335.16 |

- **Card fees take more than before.** Every family is a separate payment, so the
  30-cent part of the fee is paid on each one: about 59 cents on every $10.
- **A trainer who stays is worth far more than a month.** If 5% of trainers leave
  each month, the average trainer stays about 20 months, so a trainer with 20 paying
  families is worth about $3,700.
- **That is why spending about $220 to win a trainer is fine.** It is the figure the
  ad rules in `ads.md` use, and such a trainer pays it back in a little over a month.
- **A trainer who covers it themselves is worth about the same.** At $5 a player for
  a whole roster of 45, they pay $225 a month, against $200 from 20 paying families.
  This option is not in the scenarios.

## What it's worth to the trainer

A private session costs $80 to $125. The trainer now pays nothing, so the only
question for them is whether sessions go up or down.

**One weekly player:**

| Session price | Family pays the trainer a month | The family's $10 as a share of that |
|---|---|---|
| $80 | $320 | about 3% |
| $100 | $400 | 2.5% |
| $125 | $500 | 2% |

For a family, $10 is a small addition to what they already spend. For the trainer it
costs nothing, so any extra session is a gain.

### Sticky, or cannibalising?

For every ten weekly players at $100 a session (40 sessions and $4,000 a month):

| What happens to sessions booked | Change in the trainer's month |
|---|---|
| Down 10% (cannibalising) | -$400 |
| Down 5% | -$200 |
| No change | $0 |
| Up 5% (two extra sessions) | +$200 |
| Up 10% | +$400 |

**Why sticky is the likelier outcome:**

- The drills are homework for the session, set by the trainer. They point back to
  the next session; they don't replace the coaching, the correction or the trainer
  watching.
- A player who practised sees progress, and a parent who sees progress keeps paying.
  A parent who is paying $10 a month for the plan also has a reason to see it used.
- A trainer who sees a player fall behind can step in before that family drifts
  away. Today they find out when the player stops booking.

**What staying longer is worth** (an illustration, not a finding): if a trainer loses
8% of players a month, the average player stays about 12.5 months. If that falls to
7%, they stay about 14.3. At $100 a session that is roughly $710 more from each
player, at no cost to the trainer.

**Where cannibalising could happen:** a family on a budget stretches weekly sessions
to every two weeks because the player "has the plan". That costs the trainer $200 a
month for that player. The trainer prevents it by presenting the at-home sessions as
part of regular training, not a cheaper version of it.

Nobody knows the size of either effect yet. Ask each of your first trainers for
sessions booked per player in the month before they started and again at day 60.

**What this means for you:** a trainer who pays nothing and books more sessions has
little reason to leave, and every reason to tell families to subscribe. Proof that
sessions go up is what will let you raise the price to families later.

## The price to families

The expected case at three prices, if the same number of families subscribe:

| Price a month | Revenue, month 12 | Net, whole year (about) | You come out behind if you lose more than |
|---|---|---|---|
| $10 | $5,465 | $30,900 | |
| $12 | $6,558 | $37,900 | 17% of subscribers |
| $15 | $8,197 | $48,400 | 33% of subscribers |

- **A higher price has more room than it looks.** At $15 you could lose a third of
  your subscribers and still earn the same.
- **But the share who subscribe is the number you know least about**, and price is
  the thing most likely to move it. Start at $10, learn the share, then test.
- **The price is stored per trainer and set by you.** Once a few trainers are live,
  you can set one trainer's families to $12 or $15 and compare how many subscribe.
- **Longer plans steady the income.** A season or 12-week pass, matching the programs
  some trainers already run, would cut cancellations. It isn't built or modelled.

## What it costs

**Every month**

| Cost | Amount | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Hosting | $20 | Your launch notes (checked August 2026) |
| Database | $25 | Your launch notes (checked August 2026) |
| Domain | about $2 | My estimate |

| AI week-building | about 10 cents a paying player | My estimate |
| Card fees | 2.9% + $0.30 on each family's payment | Stripe's standard rate |

Hosting and database costs will rise with thousands of players. That matters in the
strong case and is not modelled.

**Once**

| Cost | Amount | When | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Legal review of privacy and terms | $1,000 | Month 1 | My estimate. Get a quote. |
| First ad test | $210 | Month 2 | `ads.md`, stage 1 |
| Second ad test | $700 | Month 3 | `ads.md`, stage 2. Skipped in the conservative case. |

**Not counted**

- **Your time.** The plan asks for six to eight hours a week, about 350 hours over
  the year. In the expected case that works out to about $88 an hour in year one.
  Supporting 28 trainers and several hundred paying families will take more than
  that, and families will write to you about payments in a way trainers didn't.
- **Sales tax.** You are now selling a subscription to households in many states,
  and some states tax that. Ask an accountant before the first real payment.
- Income tax, an accountant, and setting up a company if you choose to.
- Refunds and card disputes. Expect more of both with families than with trainers.
- Slow seasons. Players stop over summer and winter breaks, and families can cancel
  any time.

## What you'd need for it to be an income

At $10 a month, after card fees and running costs:

| To clear each month | Paying players | Trainers at 20 paying families each |
|---|---|---|
| $1,000 | 115 | 6 |
| $3,000 | 329 | 16 |
| $5,000 | 544 | 27 |
| $10,000 | 1,081 | 53 |
| $20,000 | 2,155 | 106 |

**For $20,000 a month in revenue** you need 2,000 paying players. That is about 99
trainers at 20 paying families each, 267 at 7.5, or 74 at 27.

**The current plan has a ceiling.** With three new trainers a month and 5% leaving,
the trainer count levels off at about 60. At 20 paying families each that is about
1,200 players and $12,000 a month. Going past it takes more new trainers a month,
fewer leaving, a higher share of families subscribing, or a higher price.

## What moves the numbers most

1. **The share of families who subscribe.** Going from 25% to 60% of a roster more
   than doubles everything. It is also the least certain number here. The parent
   message in `launch-kit.md`, and the trainer saying it in person, are what move it.
2. **Families cancelling.** At 8% a month you replace about two thirds of your paying
   families each year. Players finishing sessions every week is the early sign that they'll stay.
3. **Roster size.** A trainer with 15 players is worth a third of one with 45.
4. **Trainers leaving, and new trainers a month.** One new trainer a month barely
   outpaces the ones who leave.
5. **The price.** See above.

The founding trainers' families are free for 3 months, so the subscribing share at
day 90 comes from trainers four to ten.

- **At or above expected:** keep going, and put more time into referrals.
- **Between conservative and expected:** find which number is low and fix that before
  spending more on ads.
- **Below conservative:** the price, the audience or the product needs to change.
  You'll have spent about $1,300 to learn it.
