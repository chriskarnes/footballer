# Ads: strategy, concepts and copy

## The short version

- **Advertise to trainers, not to players or parents.** A player can only use
  Train.futbol through a trainer today, so an ad a parent sees has nowhere to send
  them. Parent ads make sense once the train-on-your-own space is open to everyone.
- **Ads are the third channel, not the first.** Your first ten trainers will come from
  people you contact yourself (see `go-to-market.md`). Run ads once one trainer is
  live with players, so the ads can show real use and a real quote.
- **Start with $15 a day on Instagram for two weeks.** About $200. That is enough to
  learn which message gets trainers to ask for a page.

## Who to target

Independent private soccer trainers in the US. On Meta (Instagram and Facebook):

| Setting | Use |
|---|---|
| Location | Start with your own metro area plus two or three others with year-round soccer. A small area lets you offer to meet in person. |
| Age | 21 to 50 |
| Interests | Soccer coaching, United Soccer Coaches, US Youth Soccer, personal trainer, small business owner. Combine "soccer" with "coach or trainer" so you don't pay for fans. |
| Placements | Instagram feed, Reels and Stories only at first |
| Later | Once 100 people have visited /trainers, add a retargeting audience. Once 20 trainers have asked for a page, build a lookalike audience from that list. |

On Google Search, target people already looking (keywords below). The volume is small,
but these are the people closest to signing up.

## Where the ad sends people

`train.futbol/trainers`, with a tag on the link so you can tell ads apart:

```
https://train.futbol/trainers?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=paid&utm_campaign=founding&utm_content=six-days
```

Change `utm_content` for each ad. The goal you count is a submitted request at `/start`.

**Privacy rule:** put an ad pixel on the marketing pages only (`/`, `/trainers`,
`/start`). Never in the player app or on a trainer's page. Children use those, and
tracking them for advertising is the kind of thing children's privacy law is written
to stop.

## Six ad concepts

Each one is a single idea. Film on a phone; these do better looking real than polished.

### 1. The other six days

- **Visual:** a week as seven boxes. One lights up ("An hour with you"). The other six
  stay empty, then fill in with short sessions and check marks.
- **Headline:** One session a week. Training all week.
- **Text:** You see each player about once a week. The ones who improve are the ones
  who get touches on the other days. Give your players drills to do at home, and see
  who did them.
- **Button:** Learn more

### 2. Did they do it?

- **Visual:** screen recording of the trainer's player list: On track, Missed 1
  session, No training in 10 days. A thumb taps "Nudge".
- **Headline:** See who trained this week
- **Text:** You give your players homework. Do you know who does it? Each player's
  week on one line, before you see them next.
- **Button:** Learn more

### 3. The text thread

- **Visual:** split screen. Left: a messy text thread of YouTube links and "did you do
  the drills?" with no answer. Right: a clean session with the video playing and
  drills checked off.
- **Headline:** Stop texting drill links
- **Text:** Your drills and your videos, in one place, in the order you want them
  done. Players check off each session and you see it.
- **Button:** Learn more

### 4. Your name on it

- **Visual:** a phone opening `yourname.train.futbol`, with a trainer's name and
  colors. Cut between two differently branded pages.
- **Headline:** Your own training page
- **Text:** Your page, your drills, your videos, your name. Players join from one link
  with no app to download. Free for you; families pay $10 a month.
- **Button:** Get started

### 5. A trainer says it (run this as soon as you have one)

- **Visual:** your first trainer, on the field, talking to the camera for 20 seconds,
  then their phone showing their players' week.
- **Headline:** "[Their one best sentence]"
- **Text:** [Trainer name] trains [N] players in [city]. Here's what changed when their
  training started going home with them.
- **Button:** Learn more

### 6. Founding trainers

- **Visual:** plain text on the dark green brand color, no footage.
- **Headline:** Your first 3 months free
- **Text:** We're setting up our first 3 private soccer trainers by hand: your page,
  your first sessions, built with you. Free for your first 3 months. After that, families
  pay $10 a month per player.
- **Button:** Apply now

Start with 1, 2 and 3. Add 5 the day you have the footage; it will probably win.

**About footage:** film ads with your first trainer's own drills, or drills you made
yourself. The drill library on the demo page is licensed for demos, not for
advertising, so keep it out of ad footage. Get written permission from a parent before
any child other than your own appears in an ad.

## Google Search ads

**Keywords** (phrase match):

- "soccer training app for coaches"
- "app for private soccer trainers"
- "soccer homework app"
- "assign soccer drills to players"
- "soccer coach app for players"
- "private soccer training software"

**Negative keywords:** free drills, jobs, near me, lessons, camp, salary, certification.

**Headlines** (30 characters or fewer):

- For Private Soccer Trainers
- Assign Drills Between Sessions
- See Who Trained This Week
- Drills Players Do at Home
- Your Own Training Page
- Families Pay $10 a Month
- No App Download for Players
- Soccer Trainer Homework App

**Descriptions** (90 characters or fewer):

- Give your players drills to do at home between sessions, and see who did them.
- Your page, your drills, your videos. Players join from one link. Families pay $10 a month.
- We set up your page and first sessions with you. No setup fee and no contract.

## Budget and rules

| Stage | When | Spend | What you're testing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Before a trainer is live | $0 | Nothing. Do outreach. |
| 1 | Weeks 5 and 6 | $15 a day on Instagram, ads 1 to 3, one audience | Which message gets requests |
| 2 | Weeks 7 to 10 | $25 a day: the winning ad, ad 5, and $5 a day on Google | Whether paid requests turn into active trainers |
| 3 | After that | Only what pays back | See the rule below |

**When to stop or keep going.** At $10 a player the family pays, not the trainer, so
what a trainer is worth to you comes from their players' families. Take a trainer with 22 players whose families
pay $10 a month: that is $220 a month. The 22 is an assumption until you have your
own numbers. If you're willing to spend about one month of that to win a trainer, an
active trainer is worth about $220 to you. If one in three requests becomes an active
trainer (also a guess), a request is worth about $70.

- An ad that has spent $60 with no request: turn it off.
- Requests cost under $70 each: keep going and raise the budget slowly.
- Requests cost over $70 after $300 in total: stop, and put the time into outreach
  and getting a trainer on camera.

Change one thing at a time (the picture, or the headline, or the audience) so you know
what made the difference. Check the numbers twice a week, not daily.
